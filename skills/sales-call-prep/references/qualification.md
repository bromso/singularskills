# Qualification, discovery and closing

How the sales skills judge a deal, run discovery, build a value case and plan the close. The rule underneath all of it: write down what the buyer actually said, label everything else, and never sell past the facts.

## Labels

- Every buyer fact is **known** (with its source: "call notes, 3 March", "email from the CFO"), **assumed** (the seller's belief, with why), or **unknown**.
- Every person on the buyer's side is **identified** (we know they exist and their role) or **engaged** (they have talked to us and taken part). An economic buyer who is only identified is not the same as one who is engaged.
- Every number is `computed` (from figures the buyer or user supplied, with the script beside it), `target`, `not measured`, or `illustration` (an example, never presented as the buyer's result).

## Qualification: MEDDPICC

Each element is labelled known, assumed or unknown:

| Element | The question |
| --- | --- |
| **Metrics** | What measurable outcome does the buyer want, in their numbers? |
| **Economic buyer** | Who can say yes to the money, and are they engaged? |
| **Decision criteria** | What will they judge the options on? |
| **Decision process** | What steps, people and dates lead to a decision? |
| **Paper process** | What happens between "yes" and a signature: legal, security review, procurement? |
| **Identified pain** | What problem, in their words, costs them enough to act? |
| **Champion** | Who inside wants this to happen and will sell it internally, and are they engaged? |
| **Competition** | What else are they considering, including doing nothing? |

**No decision is a competitor.** Many deals end with the buyer doing nothing; vendor sources put it at 40–60% of B2B deals (a vendor figure, cited secondhand from CSO Insights). Always ask what the buyer does if they do nothing, and what that costs them.

**Buying committees.** Complex purchases involve several people, typically 6–10 in Gartner's research as cited by Highspot (*Engaging B2B buying committees*, https://www.highspot.com/blog/b2b-buying-committee/). Map each: role, what they care about, stance (for, neutral, against, unknown), identified or engaged.

The qualification ends in **pursue**, **nurture** (not now, keep in touch) or **drop**, with the unknowns that would change it.

## Discovery: listen first

A meta-analysis of about 20 years of research found that customer-oriented salespeople show care through listening, which helps them adapt to what the buyer needs, and that listening drives both relationship and sales outcomes (Itani et al., 2019, *Journal of Business Research*: "Building customer relationships while achieving sales performance results: Is listening the holy grail of sales?").

- Ask open questions, one at a time, and follow up on the buyer's own words ("you said the month-end close slips — what happens when it does?").
- Ask about the situation, the problem, its impact, and the cost of doing nothing, before saying anything about the product.
- Write the buyer's pains as quotes; anything they did not say stays unknown.
- Ask how they have made similar purchases before, and who else needs to be involved.

## The value case

Tie value to the buyer's own metrics. Compute any saving or return with a script from numbers the buyer supplied (hours lost per week × people × loaded hourly cost, for example), show the inputs and their source, and give a range when an input is uncertain. Without the buyer's numbers, a figure is an **illustration** and says so.

## The mutual action plan

Deals stall after the proposal when nobody agreed the steps to signature. A **mutual action plan** works back from the buyer's go-live date, not from the seller's quarter: every step has an owner on each side and a date, and legal, security review and procurement run in parallel with the commercial steps instead of at the end (MEDDICC, *Go-Live Plan*, https://meddicc.com/resources/go-live-plan). Agree it with the buyer; a plan only the seller has seen is a forecast, not a plan.

## Honest selling

No false urgency, no fake scarcity, no misrepresenting the product or a competitor, and no commitment (discount, term, date, feature) beyond the policy the user stated. Anything beyond policy is marked **for approval**, with who approves.
