# Win-loss review

How the sales skills learn from closed deals, one at a time or many together. The central rule: the seller's account and the buyer's account are different evidence, and only the buyer can say why the buyer decided.

## Sellers misjudge why they lose

Research on failed key-account proposals collected the reasons from buyers, because data from salespeople and their managers carries attribution bias: sellers tend to explain losses by factors outside their control. The buyers named three drivers: adaptability, relationship potential and cost (Friend et al., 2014, *Industrial Marketing Management*: "Why are you really losing sales opportunities? A buyers' perspective on the determinants of key account sales failures").

Practitioner guides agree, and warn about price in particular: buyers often give price as the easy answer that ends an awkward conversation, and seller-logged loss reasons frequently differ from what buyers say in interviews (Clozd, *Win Loss Analysis: The Ultimate 2026 Guide*, https://www.clozd.com/guides/win-loss-analysis; Corporate Visions, *How to Conduct Win-Loss Analysis*, https://corporatevisions.com/blog/how-to-conduct-win-loss-analysis-implementation-guide/). Their statistics come from vendors of win-loss services and are labelled as such when used.

## Rules

- **Keep the two accounts apart.** The seller's view and the buyer's view are separate columns, never merged.
- **A reason only the seller gave is not the cause.** Label it "seller-reported" and say what buyer evidence would confirm it.
- **No buyer input.** When the user has no interview, email or debrief from the buyer, write **no buyer input**, and offer to draft an interview guide.
- **Price is checked, not accepted.** Before anyone recommends changing price, compare the seller's "price" reasons with what buyers said about the same deals.
- **Cover won, lost and no-decision deals**, not just losses, so the review is not only about what went wrong.
- **The gap is the finding.** Where the seller's and buyer's accounts differ, that difference is the most useful result.
- **Every finding gets an owner** and a next step, or it changes nothing.

## Interviewing the buyer

- Ask soon after the decision, while it is fresh: within a few weeks is common.
- A **neutral** interviewer (someone other than the seller on the deal) usually hears more candid answers; say the conversation is to improve, not to reopen the deal.
- Ask open "what" and "why" questions, and let the buyer tell the story: "Walk me through how you made the decision." "What almost changed your mind?" "What did we do that made it harder?"
- Keep it short (20–30 minutes) and never argue.

## People

Cross-deal reviews use deal or company names only; quotes from buyers drop people's names.
